\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

We separate what the paper does not show from what it shows wrongly; the first list is longer.

\paragraph{One machine, one toolchain, seven runs.}
Every cross-language number comes from a single 24-core laptop running Linux under WSL2 with gcc
15.2, rustc 1.93.1, Go 1.26.8, OpenJDK 25 and Node 24.  Runs are interleaved and reversed in parity,
and we only ever compare arms measured in the same session, because the same two binaries drift by
up to 13\% between sessions on this host.  Seven repetitions give a useable ordering of arms that
differ by more than a few percent; they do not give confidence intervals, and we report minima and
medians rather than a mean with a variance we would have to justify.  A different allocator, a
different libc, or a machine with a different cache-line and TLB geometry can move the numbers, and
\S\ref{sec:eval-crosslang} is the section where that matters most.

\paragraph{The ablation arms are compile-time constants, not product switches.}
\tab\ref{tab:ablate} changes one \code{-D} constant of the same generated C file, which is the
right unit for attributing an effect to a mechanism, but it is not the same as a released compiler
option.  Two mechanisms have no arm at all: static placement (there is no build in which an object
that escapes is nevertheless allocated in the frame) and the per-unit idle analysis (turning it off
would change which units emit a runtime, not how a given unit behaves).  The reclamation-off arm is
also conservative: it still uses the bump arena and the block-exit release, so the "cost of
reclamation" we report is the cost of the sweep and count tiers, not of the whole substrate.

\paragraph{The pointer-chasing gap is explained, not removed.}
On \code{chase} we are 1.01 times the best C++ arm at the minimum and 1.066 times at the median, and
we know where the difference lives: 3.9\% is the collector (marking and sweeping a live set that is
nearly the whole heap, which is the definition of the shape) and 1.9\% is a layout phase effect,
because a 48-byte slot steps through the four phases of a 64-byte line and a two-word field
therefore straddles a line in one phase out of four.  Padding slots to 64 bytes removes the effect
and costs 33\% more instructions and 20\% more wall clock; reordering the fields moves the phase
without changing the rate.  We chose the compact layout and the language keeps a deterministic
field order.  A reader who values the last percent of a pointer-chasing loop more than heap density
would choose otherwise, and that is a language decision, not a runtime one.

\paragraph{The admission reservation binds only an explicit wall.}
\S\ref{sec:impl} reserves room for the largest single request so that
the reservation inequality holds, but only when the user sets \code{EXTC\_RC\_M\_MAX}.  Under the default
implicit wall of three times the live set the reservation is left out by design: the implicit wall
grows with the program, so a promise of the form \mth{thr + R_{\max} \le M_{\max}} cannot be kept
by construction, and the earlier unconditional version of the rule cost 27\% more resident memory on
\code{chase} before we narrowed it.  The measured benefit of the reservation is therefore a contract
and a smaller trigger ceiling, not a lower peak; \S\ref{sec:eval-ablation} reports it as such and we
do not claim a memory win we cannot show.

\paragraph{The counting tier is almost never selected.}
Two of the 794 corpus programs that compile satisfy the acyclicity side conditions of
\S\ref{sec:tiers}.  The tier is sound and it is the one that removes the sweep entirely, so its poor
recall is a limitation of the proof and not of the mechanism: the conditions demand that every
cell that can hold a reference be counted and that every stored value be provably fresh or strictly
deeper, which is a strong ask for heap graphs built by a loop.  We report the number instead of
widening the conditions, because widening them without a proof would trade a claim we can check for
a trend we cannot.

\paragraph{Sweeping is proportional to capacity, not to the live set.}
\thm\ref{thm:pause} charges the collector for the number of words it scans, and the sweep scans the
occupancy bitmap of a region, so a region whose live set is tiny but whose capacity is large costs
what its capacity implies.  The elide counters of \S\ref{sec:impl} skip regions in which
everything is dead, which covers the common case, but a sparse region of mostly live slots is paid
for by its geometry.  Making the sweep proportional to the live set would need a different structure
(a mark-to-slot list, or mark-compact), and we did not measure that alternative.

\paragraph{Pause populations are not comparable across languages.}
The runtime does all of its work on the mutator thread, so every microsecond the collector spends is
a microsecond the program does not run, and \tab\ref{tab:pause} is the distribution of exactly that.
A concurrent collector, by construction, moves most of the same work off the mutator and reports a
pause population that is ten times smaller because it measures a different quantity
(\S\ref{sec:eval-pause} shows the arithmetic for Go from its own metrics).  The numbers in
\tab\ref{tab:pauseruntimes} should therefore be read as "what a single-threaded collector must
spend", not as "how a concurrent collector compares".  Within our own population the tail is also
host-sensitive: the noise floor of an empty 2.3\,\us window on this machine has a 99.9th percentile
of 22 to 26\,\us and one sample in the main run reached 80.1\,\us, so we treat a single worst step as
a report about the machine and only a distribution as a property of the runtime
(\S\ref{sec:eval-pause}).

\paragraph{Three instrumentation bugs were found late.}
A histogram whose last bucket was assigned instead of compared made "over one millisecond" true for
every sample; a CPU clock that silently returned zero because a feature-test macro was not declared
made every step look free; and a drain test assumed one bounded pass empties a queue that is
deliberately allowed to need several.  All three are fixed and the gate suite now fails when the
corresponding probe stops reporting (\S\ref{sec:eval-correct}).  The honest reading is that earlier
pause numbers in our own development history — including one published internal report that a
60\,\us target was met — were produced by instruments that could not have detected the fault, and we
corrected that record rather than quietly replacing it.

\paragraph{The proofs cover the model, not the compiler.}
The Lean development of \S\ref{sec:formal-status} establishes the safety invariants of the region
machine, the maximality of the idle criterion, the memory gap law and the precision of the store
rule, with no \code{sorry}.  It does not establish \thm\ref{thm:pause} (the pause theorem is a
statement about the emitted code, checked by gates and by measurement), it does not model the C
implementation, and it does not prove the escape analysis whose judgements \S\ref{sec:formal}
assumes.  The gap between "the checker accepts" and "the theorem applies" is therefore machine
checked at the leaves and paper-checked in the middle.

\paragraph{Corpus and comparison arms.}
The census of \S\ref{sec:eval-census} counts 1,396 \code{.extc} files, of which 602 do not compile
as a single file: 378 are deliberately invalid programs in negative-test directories and the rest are
multi-module or library sources that need more than one command line.  The tier percentages are over
the 794 that compile, which is the honest denominator but not a random sample of programs.  The C++,
Rust, Go, Java and Node arms were written by us from the same shape description and checked for
byte-identical output, but they are our implementations of "the best hand-written version", and a
specialist in any of those languages could move its number.

\paragraph{Concurrency is out of scope.}
Threads do not share managed regions in this design: a parallel region gives each worker a private
arena that is released as a unit when the worker finishes, which is why every theorem of
\S\ref{sec:formal} is single-threaded.  The contract we would want for \code{threads} blocks — a
per-worker version of the same two bounds, plus absence of data races from the absence of shared
mutable data — is sketched in the design notes and not proved here.
